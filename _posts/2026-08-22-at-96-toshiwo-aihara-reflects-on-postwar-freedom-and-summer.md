Toshiwo Aihara, 96, who lives in Kawana, looked at a sepia photograph of his native Ueda, Nagano, where his father worked as a tailor, and quietly recounted memories while fingering the pages of his memoir. He spoke about the contrast between the town’s simple streets and the heavy price paid for freedom in the postwar years, linking personal family history to broader reflections on Japan’s past. Aihara’s remarks underscore the enduring emotional weight of wartime experiences even 81 years after the end of the war.
